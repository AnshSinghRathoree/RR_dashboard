1. How Pressure Is Measured (PI ∈ [0, 1])

Every delivery is assigned a continuous Pressure Index before the ball is bowled, adjusting for rain-revised targets and Super Overs (fixed at 1.00):

  • Innings 2 (Chasing): 40% Required Run Rate vs. competition par + 26% non-linear wicket depletion + 24% competitive endgame compression + 10% recent wicket shock (discounted ×0.78 in hopeless blowouts).
  • Innings 1 (Setting Target): 36% death-over leverage (overs 15–20) + 34% early collapse recovery + 18% below-par scoring deficit + 12% recent wicket shock.
  • High-Pressure Regime: Top quartile (PI ≥ 0.3644); remaining 75% forms each player's normal baseline.

2. Plain-English Glossary of Metrics

  • RAPP (Runs Above Phase-Par / 100 balls): Extra runs a batter scores per 100 balls compared to the average batter in that exact tournament, innings, and match phase.
  • ΔRAPP (Clutch Elevation): High-Pressure RAPP minus Normal-Pressure RAPP. Positive values prove the batter accelerates when pressure spikes.
  • RSPP & ΔRSPP (Bowlers): Runs saved per over compared to the exact phase average under pressure, and the improvement over their own normal baseline.
  • Empirical Bayes Shrinkage: Pulls small-sample hot streaks toward the peer average (priors: k=180 balls batters, k=200 balls bowlers) so sustained reliability wins.
